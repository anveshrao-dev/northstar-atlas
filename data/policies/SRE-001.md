## 1. Purpose and Scope

### 1.1 Covered Incidents

This policy proposes how the fictional Northstar Technologies detects,
coordinates, mitigates, and reviews production reliability incidents. It covers
service disruption, degradation, credible threats to service continuity or data
integrity, and deployment failures affecting production services or their safe
restoration. A failed nonproduction build without production impact or a
credible production threat remains an engineering issue, not automatically a
production incident.

SRE coordinates reliability assessment and restoration priorities. Platform
Engineering remains the primary technical owner of platform-managed
infrastructure; application, database, and network teams retain their technical
responsibilities. This policy defines decisions and handoffs, not troubleshooting
commands, real infrastructure details, or regulatory compliance claims.

### 1.2 Draft Authority and Source Boundaries

**All SRE-specific requirements are proposed and not effective until reviewed
and approved.** “Must” denotes a proposed requirement. This draft does not
authorize a production change, privileged access, or a control bypass.

HR-001 v1.0 remains the Approved (Synthetic) source for employee lifecycle rules.
IT-001 and PLAT-001 v0.1 remain Draft — Proposed (Synthetic), used here for
compatible access and deployment designs. Acceptance of their project
deliverables does not publish them as effective policies. SEC-001 and COMP-001
are present at v0.1, Draft — Proposed (Synthetic). Their proposed controls can
be consulted; actual mechanisms, delegations, and schedules remain unresolved.

## 2. Roles and Responsibilities

The following operating model is proposed.

| Role | Responsibility and authority boundary |
| --- | --- |
| Reporter or first responder | Report observed impact and evidence; engage SRE and responsible teams; perform only actions already within their access and documented authorization |
| SRE incident coordinator | Maintain incident ownership, severity assessment, shared priorities, team engagement, communication, and restoration/closure coordination; incident coordination does not grant technical execution, change approval, or privileged-access authority |
| Platform Engineering / Cloud / DevOps | Own platform-managed Azure infrastructure, Terraform technical review and execution, cloud-hosted Jenkins infrastructure and platform-managed deployment pipelines, AKS/Kubernetes platform operations, infrastructure integrations, troubleshooting, and technical recovery |
| Application teams and service owner | Own application-code investigation, release artifacts, application fixes, application-specific validation, and assigned release/recovery work; provide business-impact and service-health evidence |
| Database teams | Own database-specific investigation and remediation, database-side connectivity, migration execution, integrity checks, and recovery tasks; coordinate application/schema dependencies |
| DNS/Network teams | Own DNS, network routing, and network-managed connectivity components; coordinate shared ingress and load-balancer boundaries with Platform |
| Information Security | Own security-incident controls and privileged-access requirements; assess suspected compromise, exposure, and security-sensitive response decisions; security response procedures remain a dependency |
| IT Operations | Own identity administration and coordinate authorized access provisioning/removal with technical owners under the proposed IT-001 model |
| Change Management | Retain production change authorization under proposed PLAT-001; assess readiness, coordination, scope, risk, and window without becoming the Terraform technical approver |
| Rapid Response | Coordinate urgent cross-team assistance and deployment-window escalation according to documented authority; participation does not grant extension, change, access, or security-exception approval |
| Compliance | Own evidence retention, review, and disposal governance; COMP-001 proposes governance, while detailed schedules remain unresolved |
| Human Resources | Retain employee lifecycle records and notification authority under HR-001; incident needs confer no deployment approval role or automatic extension to employee access |

Here, Platform infrastructure ownership and verification refer to cloud-managed
components. Data Center teams own on-premises infrastructure implementation and
verification, including on-premises Jenkins; database teams retain database-specific
administration. DNS/Network handoffs follow the actual cloud or on-premises
component owner. This terminology follows proposed SEC-001 §§2.1, 6, and 7.2;
it does not grant a new approval role or supply missing technical runbooks.

Each incident records named individuals for its coordinating and technical roles.
A person holding multiple roles must record which responsibility and documented
authority applies to each decision. Joining SRE, owning a service, or having tool
permissions is not evidence of production or privileged-access approval.

## 3. Incident Detection and Reporting

### 3.1 Reports and Initial Evidence

**Proposed:** Reports may originate from monitoring, application teams, support
channels, deployment verification, or employee observations. On observing
suspected production impact or a credible threat, report it through the
designated incident intake route and engage SRE without waiting for a confirmed
root cause. Actual channels and responder coverage must be designated before
operational adoption; this policy supplies no real contact details.

Record an incident identifier, reporter, discovery time with time-zone context,
affected services, observed symptoms and business impact, available sanitized
evidence, and any associated release, CR, or cTASK. Distinguish the first observed
symptom from the incident's estimated start; unknown facts remain marked unknown.

### 3.2 Triage and Declaration

**Proposed:** The responding SRE representative assesses the report with service
and technical owners, declares a reliability incident when §1.1 applies, and
records the rationale, provisional severity, and named coordinator. Any responder
may request declaration or escalation; a report is not itself a change approval.
Uncertain cause must not prevent recording credible impact and engaging help.

If SRE cannot be reached, the first responder escalates through designated team
contacts and Rapid Response for coordination assistance, records the ownership
gap, and continues only already-authorized actions. No new approval powers arise
from an unavailable coordinator. A report assessed as a nonincident retains its
reason and engineering follow-up owner; reconsider it if impact changes.

## 4. Incident Severity Classification

### 4.1 Proposed Qualitative Categories

**Proposed:** Classify by observed or credibly threatened business/service impact,
scope, ability to contain harm, and viability of a workaround. A technology name
or failed job alone does not determine severity.

| Category | Qualitative criteria | Coordination expectation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SEV-1 — Critical | Widespread or rapidly worsening loss of essential service, core business activity blocked without a viable workaround, or credible severe service/data-integrity harm requiring coordinated containment | SRE coordinates concentrated cross-team response and affected business leadership engagement; Security participates when security concerns are present |
| SEV-2 — Major | Material impairment to an important service or user group, with impact contained or a partial workaround available, but coordinated restoration is still required | Engage affected technical owners and service stakeholders; escalate if containment, workaround, or restoration prospects deteriorate |
| SEV-3 — Limited | Localized, contained production impairment with limited business impact and a viable workaround or stable service path | Maintain an accountable coordinator and technical owner, proportionate updates, verification, and follow-up; escalate if impact expands |

These are proposed internal labels, not numerical response-time SLAs or universal
availability thresholds. Security incident classification is a separate Security
responsibility and must not be inferred from this reliability scale.

### 4.2 Assessment and Reclassification

**Proposed:** The SRE coordinator records severity with service-owner impact
input and technical evidence. Use a provisional category when impact is uncertain,
state the uncertainty, and reassess as evidence develops. Where multiple criteria
apply, use the highest applicable impact category and record why.

Record severity changes, reasons, and times; communicate them to responders and
stakeholders. A downgrade requires evidence of reduced impact, not merely a
successful job or lack of new alerts. Severity does not select an emergency
change route, confer access, or approve a deployment-window extension.

## 5. Incident Ownership and Coordination

### 5.1 Named Coordinator and Shared Priorities

**Proposed:** SRE assigns one named incident coordinator and identifies technical
leads for each affected area. The coordinator maintains the incident record,
shared impact assessment, restoration priorities, task owners, dependencies,
communication checkpoints, and unresolved decisions. Actual SRE assignment and
delegation arrangements require review before adoption.

Technical leads propose and validate their actions. SRE coordinates their
sequence and conflicting work against service impact; it does not acquire
ownership of every fix. Disputes about technical scope, risk, authorization, or
resource ownership are recorded and escalated to the responsible owners and,
where needed, Rapid Response, Change Management, or Security. Coordination
cannot override those authorities.

### 5.2 Handoffs and Security Overlap

**Proposed:** A coordinator or technical-owner handoff records the outgoing and
incoming owners, acknowledgment, time, current impact, active tasks, authorization
limits, pending decisions, and next communication checkpoint. An assignment
message without acknowledgment is not a completed handoff; an unavailable owner
or unaccepted handoff remains visible and is escalated.

If suspected compromise or exposure accompanies an outage, involve Security
and link its response record to the reliability incident. SRE coordinates
service restoration while Security owns security-incident controls. Resolve
conflicting restoration and containment proposals through documented authority;
do not restore service by silently overriding a Security restriction. Detailed
joint-command and security-response procedures remain open dependencies.

## 6. Technical Investigation and Team Engagement

### 6.1 Scenario Ownership and Handoffs

**Proposed:** Engage owners based on the affected component and evidence, not
on the assumption that every production symptom belongs to SRE.

| Incident context | Technical ownership and collaboration |
| --- | --- |
| Jenkins deployment failures affecting production releases | Platform investigates cloud-hosted Jenkins infrastructure and platform-managed deployment pipelines; Data Center teams investigate on-premises Jenkins infrastructure; application teams investigate build, code, artifact, and application-release failures. Record source/target owners for cross-hosting handoffs under proposed SEC-001 §7.2. SRE assesses production impact and coordinates priorities |
| AKS pod crashes, OOMKilled, or memory pressure | Application teams investigate application behavior and resource requirements; Platform investigates cluster configuration, capacity, and platform-managed resources. SRE coordinates reliability assessment across both |
| Ingress, NGINX, or session-affinity problems | Platform owns platform-managed ingress/NGINX configuration; network owners address network-managed routing components; application teams validate session behavior and compatibility |
| Azure Key Vault secret retrieval failures | Platform investigates workload identity and platform integration; applications validate secret-reference use. IT and Security handle affected access decisions; teams do not expose secret values while collecting evidence |
| DNS or load-balancer connectivity issues | DNS/Network teams investigate DNS and network-managed routing/load balancers; Platform investigates platform-managed load balancers and integration. Record the actual component owner and cross-team handoff |
| Venafi certificate issuance or renewal failures | Platform coordinates infrastructure-integration investigation with the designated certificate-service owner and Security; applications validate effects on service connectivity. The certificate-service owner and lifecycle procedures remain to be confirmed |
| Database connectivity or migration failures | Database teams own database-side diagnosis, migration, integrity, and recovery work; applications own code/schema compatibility and application behavior; engage Platform and network teams for infrastructure dependencies |
| Failed production deployment needing rollback or forward recovery | Platform performs platform-managed recovery; application, database, and network teams perform their assigned recovery tasks. SRE coordinates restoration priorities; CR and recovery authorization remain governed by PLAT-001 |

This aligns with proposed PLAT-001 §9.3. Detailed diagnostic and recovery
runbooks should be separate later corpus additions; none is created or assumed
available here. These examples do not assert that live integrations exist.

### 6.2 Coordinated Investigation

**Proposed:** Technical owners record hypotheses separately from verified facts,
proposed actions, dependencies, expected effects, and results. Share sanitized
findings through the incident record. Coordinate potentially conflicting actions
and identify an executor before any authorized production intervention.

Unclear ownership requires explicit engagement and escalation, not repeated
unaccepted ticket transfers. Consult the CR/cTASK and component ownership
records. Evidence collection itself remains subject to access and Security
controls; a diagnostic label does not authorize privileged actions.

## 7. Escalation and Rapid Response Coordination

### 7.1 Escalation Triggers and Routing

**Proposed:** Escalate when impact grows, a workaround fails, the responsible
team cannot be reached, ownership or authority is unclear, investigations stall
without a credible next action, teams conflict, a security concern emerges, or
the deployment window is at risk. Do not wait for an invented numerical timer.

SRE coordinates engagement of the relevant technical leads and service owner.
Use Rapid Response for urgent assistance or cross-team blockers, Change
Management for change-governance decisions, and Security for security or access
decisions. Record the requested help or decision, impact, owner, response, and
unresolved items. Actual contact rosters, backup coverage, and fallback escalation
routes must be established; unavailable approval is not implied approval.

### 7.2 Rapid Response and Window Requests

**Proposed:** Rapid Response gathers urgent technical assistance, coordinates
cross-team escalation, and routes deployment-window extension requests in line
with proposed PLAT-001 §11.3. During a declared reliability incident, the named
SRE coordinator retains incident coordination unless a documented handoff occurs.
Rapid Response participation does not automatically replace that coordinator.

For a window request, record current state, remaining tasks, requested revised
window, dependencies, risks, and safe-stop/recovery options in the linked CR.
An extension requires the recorded decision of an authorized window approver,
including authority, conditions, and revised window before relying on it.
Rapid Response coordinates the request; SRE supplies reliability input. Neither
role automatically grants an extension or Security exception.

The exact window decision-maker, Rapid Response's approval authority (if any),
delegations, and incident-command interface remain explicit governance decisions.
This draft grants no such approval powers. Without authorized extension, follow
the existing window and documented safe-stop or authorized recovery conditions;
urgency or an open request is not permission to continue beyond them.

## 8. Communication and Stakeholder Updates

### 8.1 Internal Updates

**Proposed:** The SRE coordinator names a communication owner, which may be the
coordinator for a limited incident, and identifies affected service stakeholders.
Record an initial impact summary, provisional severity, coordinating and technical
owners, known facts, uncertainties, current actions, and the next update checkpoint.

Set checkpoints proportionate to impact and active decisions, and update on
material impact/severity changes, major mitigation results, authorization blockers,
restoration, and closure. Record any revised checkpoint rather than silently
missing it. Do not invent a restoration estimate; distinguish estimates from
commitments. No fixed update interval is established by this policy.

### 8.2 Sensitive and External Communications

**Proposed:** Restrict sensitive evidence to appropriately authorized recipients.
Do not include credentials, secret values, or unnecessary personal information
in broad incident updates. Coordinate security-sensitive messages with Security.

External/customer communications require the organization's designated
communications authority and reviewed content. Its identity, channels, and
approval workflow remain to be agreed; neither SRE nor Rapid Response receives
automatic authority to publish externally. This policy supplies no legal
notification deadline or claim of regulatory compliance.

## 9. Mitigation and Service Restoration

### 9.1 Selecting and Executing Recovery

**Proposed:** SRE coordinates restoration priorities with the service owner and
technical leads. The responsible teams assess mitigation, rollback, forward
recovery, and safe-stop options for scope, dependencies, data integrity, risk,
verification, and required authorization. Record why the selected action is
appropriate and which team executes each step.

Use preauthorized recovery only within the original CR's recorded bounds and
conditions, consistent with proposed PLAT-001 §10.1. Otherwise obtain the
applicable new authorization or separately approved emergency route under §10
below. Platform performs infrastructure recovery; application, database, and
network teams retain their assigned work. SRE's restoration priority is not
permission to execute an unapproved action.

Preserve PLAT-001 §10.2's recovery limitations: Helm rollback does not guarantee
reversal of external database changes, and Terraform state restoration alone
does not restore infrastructure or lost data. A workaround may reduce impact
without resolving the underlying cause; record that distinction.

### 9.2 Verification, Restoration, and Closure

**Proposed:** Platform verifies infrastructure health; application/service owners
verify affected user functions and application health; database and network
owners verify their affected components and integrity concerns. SRE combines
this evidence against agreed service-specific restoration and observation
criteria. Record those criteria and remaining uncertainty; no universal health
threshold or observation duration is imposed.

A successful Jenkins job, Terraform apply, or quiet alert stream alone does not
establish restoration. Record the restored service scope, actual production
state, checks performed, outcomes, and residual impact. Partial restoration
must be identified as partial and communicated; it is not full resolution.

The SRE coordinator records closure after responsible owners confirm restoration
against the agreed criteria, residual risks/workarounds have documented owner
acceptance within their authority, outstanding actions have owners, and a review
owner and plan are recorded. Communicate closure and record its rationale and
time. Root-cause investigation may continue as tracked work; unresolved required
Security decisions cannot be waived through incident closure. If impact recurs,
reopen or link a new incident with its relationship and rationale recorded.

## 10. Emergency Changes and Access Dependencies

### 10.1 Production Change Authorization

**Proposed:** Incident declaration and severity do not bypass proposed PLAT-001's
standard, normal, or emergency change classifications. For ordinary production
changes, required implementation plans, completed prerequisites, nonproduction
evidence, and applicable application/technical/Security/readiness approvals
precede final Change Management CR approval under PLAT-001 §§4.1–4.4 and 8.
An incident or a cTASK assignment is not an alternative approval route.

Platform retains Terraform technical approval under PLAT-001 §6.1; Change
Management does not technically approve plans. Plan regeneration or drift
requires Platform investigation and renewed technical approval. No separate
Change Management approval is required solely for regeneration if the resulting
scope, risk, window, and other conditions remain within the authorized CR.
Material scope/risk expansion or a window overrun requires stopping affected
execution and following amendment/escalation before proceeding.

An expedited emergency change requires documented authority under Change
Management governance, minimum justified scope, applicable technical and
Security decisions, verification, and recovery evidence. Emergency delegations,
acceptable abbreviated preparation, and follow-up arrangements still require
joint agreement with Platform, Change Management, and Security under PLAT-001
§§11.1–11.2. This draft does not make an executable emergency route available,
waive protected pipeline gates, or make retrospective documentation an approval
of an earlier unapproved action.

### 10.2 Identity, Privileged Access, and Employee Lifecycle

**Proposed:** Route required access through IT and the owning technical team;
Security owns sensitive/privileged access decisions under the proposed IT-001
§§5.2, 9, and 10 model. SRE supplies incident context and justification, not
automatic access approval. A deployment approval does not grant credentials.

Emergency approvers, privileged duration, MFA controls, credential handling,
and revocation verification require agreed Security controls and IT coordination.
SEC-001 §§4, 8–10 propose controls but leave concrete mechanisms and emergency
authority unresolved; no break-glass entitlement, MFA bypass, token lifetime,
or credential rotation interval is invented here. Follow-up verifies authorized
temporary-access expiry through IT/technical owners rather than assuming
incident closure itself revokes access.

HR-001 remains authoritative for lifecycle notifications and access deadlines.
Neither an incident, a responder handoff, nor a window extension delays employee
termination disablement under HR-001 §5.2. Arrange authorized replacement
coverage without retaining a departing responder's access beyond that deadline.
Service/workload ownership is separate under proposed IT-001 §8.3; continued
service operation does not justify retaining personal access.

## 11. Incident Evidence and Timelines

**Proposed:** Maintain an attributable timeline of discovery, estimated onset,
declaration, severity changes, ownership/handoffs, hypotheses and confirmed
findings, decisions and authorities, attempted actions and results, escalations,
updates, restoration, and closure. Record times with time-zone context and mark
unknown or estimated times rather than inventing precision.

Link the incident to relevant CRs/cTASKs, artifact/plan references, access requests,
Security records, verification, window decisions, and follow-up actions. Record
who actually executed each action and what resulted; a request is not execution
evidence. Preserve corrections with attribution rather than silently rewriting
the chronology.

Use sanitized summaries and access-controlled evidence references. Never include
secret values, credentials, or raw sensitive plan/state data in ordinary updates
or tickets. Compliance owns retention, evidence review, and disposal governance;
COMP-001 §§4 and 11–13 propose governance, including hold handling; actual
schedules and designated authorities remain unresolved. This draft specifies
no retention duration, legal hold procedure, or audit certification.

## 12. Post-incident Review

### 12.1 Review Scope and Participation

**Proposed:** Each declared incident receives a recorded review proportionate to
impact and learning needs. The SRE coordinator assigns a review owner and records
the planned review checkpoint; there is no universal numerical review deadline.
Engage affected technical and service owners, with Security and Change Management
where their decisions or controls were involved.

Review impact, timeline, contributing factors, mitigation effectiveness, technical
and authorization handoffs, communication, and verification. Distinguish supported
causal findings from hypotheses and identify missing evidence. Focus on system
and process improvement without unsupported personal blame. Closure does not
require pretending the root cause is already known.

### 12.2 Corrective Actions and Follow-through

**Proposed:** Record corrective actions with accountable owners, priority,
agreed target dates or checkpoints, and completion evidence. Technical actions
remain with their owning teams; SRE tracks cross-team reliability follow-through
and escalates blocked or overdue actions. Verification, not task assignment,
establishes completion.

Propose updates to monitoring, readiness criteria, ownership records, and separate
runbooks where evidence supports them. Policy/control amendments need their own
review and approval. A post-incident review neither retroactively authorizes
actions nor creates new production access or deployment authority.

## 13. Exceptions

**Proposed:** SRE-specific exceptions require the affected requirement, reason,
scope, risk, mitigations, owner, expiry, approving authority, and expiry action
in a recorded decision. The Head of SRE may approve only exceptions within
documented SRE authority; affected control owners must review their requirements.
Security concurrence is required where security controls are affected; SRE
approval alone cannot waive them.

An exception cannot replace Change Management authorization, Platform technical
approval, or IT access decisions; grant an emergency bypass or window extension;
extend HR termination deadlines; or invent Compliance retention rules. Rapid
Response coordination is not exception approval. This proposed process remains
ineffective until reviewed and adopted.

## 14. Policy Ownership and Review

**Proposed:** The Head of SRE owns this policy and coordinates review with
Platform, application/service owners, database and network teams, IT, Security,
Change Management, Rapid Response representatives, and Compliance. Review is
annual and triggered by material incident lessons, operating-model changes,
or relevant companion-policy changes.

Security reviews security-sensitive provisions. An authorized technology
governance approver approves publication after required reviews; the actual
approver and authority remain to be confirmed. Assign an effective date only
after approval. This is a proposed publication process, with no invented
committee or assertion of current publication approval.

Resolve dependent controls before operational use; publishing SRE-001 alone
would not approve missing Security, change-delegation, or evidence requirements.
