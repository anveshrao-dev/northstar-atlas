## 1. Purpose and Draft Authority

This policy proposes how the fictional Northstar Technologies manages employee
and nonhuman identities, evaluates access requests, provisions authorized
permissions, and records access changes.

**All IT-specific requirements in this document are proposed and are not
effective until reviewed and approved.** “Must” describes a proposed
requirement, not an already approved company rule. Passages labeled
**Existing authority** summarize HR-001 v1.0 and retain that document's
authority. This draft does not amend HR-001.

PLAT-001, SRE-001, SEC-001, and COMP-001 are present in this corpus at v0.1,
Draft — Proposed (Synthetic). References identify proposed ownership and
controls; they do not establish approved procedures or permission to act.
Document presence does not resolve outstanding implementation or approval decisions.

## 2. Scope

The proposed scope covers employee accounts, standard business applications,
cloud and platform access requests, and service or workload identities used
by company applications and automation. A service or workload identity is a
nonhuman identity used by software, such as a CI/CD pipeline.

Contractor and external-partner lifecycle processes are outside this draft's
scope and must not be inferred from the employee process. Deployment approval,
incident procedures, detailed privileged-access controls, and audit retention
schedules belong to the corresponding companion policies. This document makes
no claim of compliance with a regulatory standard.

## 3. Responsibilities and Approval Boundaries

The following allocation is proposed for IT-001. Existing HR responsibilities
remain governed by HR-001.

| Actor | Responsibility | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Human Resources | Maintain employee lifecycle records and communicate lifecycle events to IT Operations | HR-001 governs transfer and offboarding notifications; HR does not approve production deployments or privileged access |
| Employee or requesting owner | Describe the identity, business purpose, requested permissions, and intended duration | A request is not an approval |
| Employee manager | Approve the business need; the receiving manager sponsors access for a role change | Business approval does not waive security controls or grant permissions |

| Compliance | Own audit and evidence governance | COMP-001 proposes governance; actual retention periods and review schedules remain unresolved |

## 4. Identity Lifecycle Management

### 4.1 Registration and Activation

**Proposed:** Each employee must have an individually attributable identity
linked to a confirmed HR employee record. For onboarding, IT must verify the
record, manager, and recorded start date before activation and must not activate
access before that start date. This onboarding rule is new; HR-001 does not
define a new-hire notification deadline.

No account or entitlement may be created solely because of a job title.
Nonhuman identities follow §8 and must not be represented as employee accounts.

### 4.2 Identity and Entitlement Records

**Proposed:** IT must maintain a record of identity type, accountable owner,
account status, target systems, approved access scope, request and approval
references, and any agreed expiry. Temporary access must have an explicit end
time; ongoing access must have a documented continuing purpose and owner.

Changes in role, ownership, purpose, or required permissions trigger a review
of affected entitlements. This draft sets no periodic review interval and no
numerical provisioning service-level target.

## 5. Access Requests and Standard Provisioning

### 5.1 Request Information

**Proposed:** A request must identify the person or workload, requesting owner,
manager or accountable business owner, target service and environment, required
permission scope, business justification, intended start, and temporary end
time or continuing purpose. Cloud requests must identify the intended scope
through the internal request system; credentials must never be included.

IT must identify the technical owner and whether the requested permissions
are standard, sensitive, privileged, or unclear. Unclear requests require
clarification before provisioning.

### 5.2 Separate Decisions

**Proposed:** The request record must distinguish these decisions:

1. **Business approval:** The employee's manager, receiving manager for a role
   change, or accountable business owner for a workload approves the need.
   The requester cannot approve their own request.
2. **Security approval:** Information Security must approve sensitive or
   privileged requests and their security conditions. Routine access may use
   a standard entitlement profile previously reviewed by Security and the
   technical owner; this does not imply that such a catalogue already exists.
3. **Technical validation and provisioning:** IT or the owning technical team
   confirms that the requested scope matches the recorded approvals and
   implements only those permissions. Provisioning capability is not approval
   authority. A changed scope must return for the affected approvals.

Business approval alone is insufficient to provision sensitive or privileged
access. Missing approval, unresolved classification, or undefined required
security controls means the request is not ready for provisioning.

### 5.3 Standard Access Fulfillment

**Proposed:** IT Operations maintains the central standard-access catalogue.
Platform Engineering owns technical permission mappings for platform-managed
resources. Information Security reviews security classifications and associated
controls. A standard profile must not be treated as approved until its required
Security and technical-owner reviews are recorded.

IT provisions standard access using an approved entitlement
profile and the narrowest scope needed for the approved purpose. It records
the executor, time, target, permissions applied, and verification result.
Completion requires confirming the resulting access and recording any failed
or partial change; submitting a request or assigning a ticket is not completion.

A new engineer's Azure development request follows this workflow and the
Platform handoff in §9.1. A development label does not automatically make every
permission standard or exempt it from Security assessment.

## 6. Role-Change Access Modifications

### 6.1 Lifecycle Record and Notification

**Existing authority — HR-001 §§4.1–4.2:** A role change requires receiving-manager
approval and an HR record before the transfer effective date. HR notifies IT
at least **two business days before** a planned internal transfer. For urgent
transfers without advance notice, HR notifies IT as soon as the change is
approved and identifies the request as urgent.

HR-001 remains authoritative for these notifications. IT-001 adds no new HR
notice period and does not define a different business-day calendar.

### 6.2 Previous and New Access

**Existing authority — HR-001 §§4.3–4.4:** IT reviews previous-role access.
Access no longer required must be removed **no later than the end of the
transfer's effective business day**. Temporary exceptions follow IT-001 and
applicable security policies; this draft does not itself approve an exception.

New access is not granted automatically based on job title. The receiving
manager submits a documented request identifying systems and business
justification. Privileged production access also refers to SEC-001, whose
requirements are present as proposals, not effective controls.

### 6.3 Coordinated Fulfillment and Completion

**Proposed:** IT coordinates any Platform-owned access changes and requests
SRE operational context when a transfer affects reliability or incident duties.
The receiving manager's request must distinguish previous access to remove
from new access to assess. Joining the SRE team does not automatically confer
production or administrative permissions.

IT tracks technical handoffs and their results against the HR effective date.
A delayed new-access request does not, by itself, authorize retaining
unnecessary previous-role access. Proposed exceptions are addressed in §12.

**Existing authority — HR-001 §4.5:** Administrative completion requires the HR
lifecycle record update, IT acknowledgment, previous-role access review,
recorded access changes, and evidence retention according to COMP-001.
Administrative completion does not itself authorize privileged access.

## 7. Access Revocation and Offboarding

### 7.1 Authoritative Notification and Disablement Times

**Existing authority — HR-001 §§5.1–5.2:** HR notifies IT at least **two business
days before planned departure, where practicable**. IT disables employee access
**no later than the approved termination effective time**. For urgent
involuntary termination, HR and IT coordinate disablement at the approved
separation time.

This draft does not substitute an end-of-day deadline, add a grace period,
or make access disablement conditional on equipment return.

### 7.2 Revocation Coordination and Verification

**Proposed:** IT identifies employee access across the central identity system,
applications, and cloud or platform services and coordinates removal with each
technical owner. Where supported, revocation verification includes active
sessions and issued tokens; disabling the primary account must not be assumed
to invalidate every downstream session.

IT records execution results and unresolved access. A blocked or failed removal
must be escalated to the technical owner and Security and recorded as incomplete.
Escalation does not extend HR-001's deadline. Equipment return remains a
manager/IT responsibility under HR-001 §5.3.

Service ownership is handled separately under §8.3. Continued operation of a
service identity does not justify retaining the departing employee's access.

## 8. Service and Workload Identities

### 8.1 Ownership and Authorization

**Proposed:** Every service or workload identity must have a named accountable
business owner, technical owner, documented purpose, environment, permission
scope, and lifecycle record. Creation and permission changes follow §5 using
the workload's business owner in place of an employee manager.

A CI/CD pipeline uses a purpose-specific nonhuman identity. Its permissions
must be limited to the approved targets and actions. Personal employee
credentials must not be used as a pipeline's standing authentication mechanism.
Possession of a deployment identity does not approve a production change.

### 8.2 Authentication and Credential Handling

**Proposed:** Prefer managed identity or workload federation where supported.
If a credential is necessary, the technical owner must record the reason and
obtain Security review of its handling and lifecycle before use. Credentials
must be stored in an approved secret-management mechanism and must not appear
in policy documents, source code, request records, or logs.

This draft does not establish credential rotation intervals, token lifetimes,
or provider-specific setup steps. Security requirements and platform
implementation details remain dependencies on SEC-001 and PLAT-001.

### 8.3 Ownership Changes and Retirement

**Proposed:** A departing or transferring owner's service identities must be
identified and assigned to a confirmed replacement owner or scheduled for
retirement by the technical owner. Do not automatically disable an otherwise
required shared service solely because its human owner leaves.

Unresolved ownership must be escalated to the technical owner and Security
for an explicit continuation or retirement decision. This is not permission
for indefinite ownerless use or a delay to employee revocation. Suspected
credential exposure also requires Security assessment. No fixed rotation
deadline or incident procedure is invented here.

## 9. Cloud and Platform Access Handoffs

### 9.1 IT and Platform Engineering

**Proposed:** IT owns employee identity administration and standard identity or
group changes within its authority. Platform Engineering validates cloud and
deployment-platform permission mappings and implements platform-owned grants
and removals. The request record must identify the executor for each action
and contain the completion result.

For Azure development access, the handoff identifies the environment, requested
scope, and business purpose. It does not grant production access by association.
This policy assigns no real subscription, cluster, tenant, role-binding, or
infrastructure identifier and specifies no deployment procedure.

### 9.2 SRE and Production Responsibilities

**Proposed:** SRE supplies reliability and incident-response context where the
requested access affects those responsibilities. Technical access, assignment
to an operational role, and approval of a production change are separate
decisions. This policy authorizes neither a deployment nor a change in incident
command.

Production change governance is proposed in PLAT-001; incident response and
escalation governance is proposed in SRE-001. Actual delegations and dependent
implementation procedures remain to be agreed.

## 10. Privileged and Emergency Access Referrals

### 10.1 Privileged or Sensitive Requests

**Proposed:** IT routes privileged or sensitive requests to Security. A
production AKS request must be routed for Security assessment of its actual
permissions and scope; it must not be treated as routine development access.
Assessment determines classification rather than assuming every production
permission is administrative.

Business justification, required Security approval, and technical execution
remain separate under §5.2. SEC-001 proposes privileged-access and MFA
requirements. Its presence does not establish an approved MFA method,
just-in-time duration, or privileged entitlement.

### 10.2 Emergency Requests

**Proposed:** Urgency is not automatic authorization. An emergency request is
routed to Security for access authorization requirements and, when incident
related, to SRE for incident coordination. The owning technical team performs
any authorized access action and records its link to the request or incident.

Emergency approver roles, temporary access duration, control exceptions, and
post-incident review requirements must be agreed in SEC-001 and SRE-001 before
this draft can support an executable emergency-access process. No default
approval, MFA bypass, or emergency access entitlement is created by these
references. Existing HR-001 urgent-termination coordination remains applicable.

## 11. Access Logging and Audit Evidence

**Proposed:** Each access action must be traceable to its subject or workload,
business purpose, requested and applied scope, applicable business and
Security decisions, technical executor, execution time with time-zone context,
verification outcome, and any expiry or exception reference.

IT and technical owners retain provisioning and revocation evidence and make
it available to authorized reviewers. Evidence access is limited to justified
roles; logs and tickets must not contain passwords, tokens, or secret values.
Recording time-zone context does not redefine HR-001's business-day calendar.

**Existing authority — HR-001 §§4.5 and 5.4:** Lifecycle access evidence and
offboarding records are retained according to COMP-001. Its retention
durations, review frequency, and actual disposal authority remain unresolved;
COMP-001 §§4 and 11–13 propose governance without adopted schedules or designated
authorities. IT-001 supplies no substitute numeric schedule.

## 12. Exceptions

**Proposed:** IT-specific exceptions, including temporary previous-role access
retention contemplated by HR-001 §4.3, require a documented scope, business
reason, risk, requester, manager or accountable business-owner approval,
mitigations, responsible technical owner, and explicit end time.

The Director of IT Operations may approve only IT-specific exceptions within
their authority. IT approval alone cannot waive Security requirements.
Security concurrence is additionally required when security controls,
sensitive access, or privileged access are affected. Exception records must
identify the approving authority, scope, expiry, relevant Security concurrence,
decision, and expiry action. This process is not effective while IT-001 is draft.

This exception process cannot change HR notification or termination times.
Employee termination deadlines cannot be extended through an IT exception.
It cannot authorize a privileged-control bypass or substitute for the future emergency
process in §10.2. HR notification exceptions remain governed by HR-001 §6;
its unspecified “relevant approval” is not assigned to a new role here.

## 13. Policy Ownership and Review

**Proposed:** The Director of IT Operations owns IT-001 and coordinates review
with HR, Platform Engineering, SRE, Security, and Compliance. Review occurs
annually and after material changes to identity services, lifecycle processes,
or relevant companion policies.

Under the proposed publication process, Information Security reviews
security-sensitive provisions. An authorized technology governance approver
approves publication after the required reviews. An effective date is assigned
only after publication approval. The specific approver and their authority
remain to be confirmed; this publication process is not yet approved.

The draft remains version 0.1 and not effective until the policy decisions,
approval authority, publication workflow, and effective date are explicitly
agreed. An approved release must record its approval and effective date.
Unresolved companion-policy requirements must be resolved before using
processes that depend on them; publication of IT-001 alone cannot approve the
missing controls.
