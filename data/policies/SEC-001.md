## 1. Purpose and Scope

### 1.1 Hybrid Infrastructure Coverage

This policy proposes privileged-access and MFA controls for the fictional
Northstar Technologies across cloud-hosted and on-premises infrastructure.
It covers workforce administration, human privileged access, service/workload
identities, credentials, and PKI/certificate governance, including Azure, GCP,
Kubernetes, Windows/Active Directory, Linux, Jenkins, network, and database
administration. Hosting location does not remove security requirements.

Security owns standards and risk decisions; IT Operations/IAM owns enterprise
identity governance; delegated technical teams implement controls on their
systems. This policy defines requirements and handoffs, not real credentials,
resource identifiers, configuration commands, or regulatory compliance claims.
It does not invent a contractor joiner/mover/leaver process absent from HR-001
and IT-001; any external-user access needs separately agreed identity governance.

### 1.2 Draft Authority and Dependencies

**All SEC-specific requirements are proposed and not effective until reviewed
and approved.** “Must” denotes a proposed requirement, not current permission
to provision access, change production, or invoke an exception.

HR-001 v1.0 remains Approved (Synthetic) and authoritative for employee lifecycle
rules. IT-001, PLAT-001, and SRE-001 v0.1 remain Draft — Proposed (Synthetic).
Their accepted project deliverables are compatible design sources, not published
operational policies. COMP-001 is also present at v0.1, Draft — Proposed
(Synthetic); its schedules and designated authorities remain unresolved. This draft supplies
new Security proposals without modifying or silently expanding existing policies.

### 2.2 Explicit Delegated Administration

**Proposed:** IAM maintains a delegation record identifying the administering
team and accountable people, permitted identity/group/permission actions, object
and environment scope, approved technical mappings, Security conditions, approval
references, review/expiry conditions, logging, and withdrawal procedure. IAM and
Security must explicitly approve the delegation before it is relied upon.

Platform may create or administer cloud identities and groups within that record.
Data Center teams may administer their identity integrations and directory
infrastructure within recorded authority. Enterprise workforce records and group
lifecycle governance remain with IAM, including where groups synchronize across
cloud and on-premises systems. Location alone does not determine governance.

Delegation permits specified technical actions; it does not approve arbitrary
entitlements, self-approval, further delegation, or Security exceptions. Each
grant still needs the applicable §5 decisions. Changed scope or ownership requires
review of the delegation and affected access. No actual delegation, role map,
catalogue profile, or unrestricted administrator entitlement is created here.

## 3. Access Risk Classification

### 3.1 Assess Effective Capabilities

**Proposed:** Security determines risk classification with IAM and the technical
owner using actual permissions, target environment, data exposure, ability to
change controls or grant access, and potential impact. Examine inherited and
group-based permissions as well as direct assignments.

| Classification | Meaning and treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Standard | Bounded routine access matching a reviewed profile in IAM's central catalogue; required Security and technical-owner reviews must be recorded under proposed IT-001 §5.3 |
| Sensitive | Access exposing sensitive information, credentials, keys, or security-relevant functions even without broad administration; requires recorded Security authorization |
| Privileged | Capability to administer systems, alter security/identity settings, assign permissions, act as other identities, or materially affect protected services; requires recorded Security authorization and the privileged controls in this policy |
| Unclear | Effective permissions or impact cannot yet be established; clarify with the owners before provisioning rather than assume standard access |

Sensitive and privileged classifications may overlap; apply all relevant
controls. A production AKS request requires Security assessment under proposed
IT-001 §10.1, but not every production permission is automatically administrative.
An Azure/GCP role name, development label, or SRE membership is insufficient to
determine risk or authorize a grant.

### 3.2 Least Privilege and Attribution

**Proposed:** Restrict access to the approved actions, resources, environment,
and purpose. Evaluate permission combinations and escalation paths; avoid broad
administrator grants where narrower permissions meet the need. Human privileged
activity must be individually attributable and separated from routine use through
the approved administrative identity or elevation design.

Prefer bounded temporary elevation for troubleshooting. Continuing privileged
access needs recorded continuing justification, owner, and review conditions;
this policy sets no universal access duration or review interval. Service
identities must not conceal human administration or replace named user accounts.

## 4. MFA Requirements

### 4.1 Human Privileged Authentication

**Proposed:** Human privileged access requires MFA through a Security-reviewed
authentication path across cloud, on-premises, and administrative consoles.
Business approval or a trusted network location does not remove this requirement.
Prefer phishing-resistant methods where supported; the approved methods,
enrollment, recovery, factor replacement, and enforcement design require Security
review and recorded adoption before use.

IAM administers workforce MFA and recovery processes, with technical teams
implementing approved integrations under delegation. Verify identity and authority
before changing factors or recovery settings; a request to resolve an outage is
not sufficient evidence. Record changes and assess suspected factor compromise
with Security. Neither administrators nor responders may silently weaken controls
to restore access.

### 4.2 Workloads and Unsupported Systems

**Proposed:** Noninteractive workloads use the identity and credential controls
in §§7–8 rather than simulated human MFA. Human administration of those workloads
still requires the applicable human MFA and privileged-access controls.

If a legacy or on-premises system cannot enforce the required MFA path, the
technical owner must obtain a reviewed integration design or a documented
Security exception under §14 with compensating controls before privileged use.
Technical inability does not silently authorize password-only access. Emergency
conditions follow §9.2; this draft defines no automatic MFA bypass or approved
fallback mechanism.

## 5. Privileged Access Requests and Approvals

### 5.1 Request Record

**Proposed:** Record the requester and subject identity/type, business or workload
owner, requested permissions and environment, cloud/on-premises component owner,
business purpose, risk assessment, intended start and explicit temporary end time
or continuing justification, authentication controls, executor, and required
delegation. Link relevant incident, access, and CR/cTASK records without including
credentials. A request is not approval or proof of provisioning.

### 5.2 Distinct Decisions and Verified Fulfillment

**Proposed:** Preserve these decisions separately:

1. **Business approval:** The employee manager, receiving manager for a transfer,
   or accountable business owner for a workload approves the need. A requester
   cannot approve their own request; actual alternate approvers need documented
   delegation rather than an assumed substitute.
2. **Security authorization:** An authorized Security reviewer approves sensitive
   or privileged scope, risks, MFA/authentication conditions, and applicable
   safeguards. Standard profiles follow §3.1; they are not a shortcut for an
   unassessed privileged request. Record the decision and approving authority.
3. **Technical validation and provisioning:** IAM coordinates identity governance;
   the appropriate delegated Cloud Platform, Data Center, database, or IAM
   administrator validates mappings and implements only authorized permissions.
   Record actual scope, executor, time, verification, expiry controls, and failures.
4. **Production change authorization:** Obtain the separate Change Management
   decision where production change control applies, consistent with proposed
   PLAT-001 §§4.1–4.4. Security access approval is not a deployment approval.

An administrator cannot self-authorize their requested access merely because
they can implement it. Missing decisions, unclear delegation, or unmet required
controls block provisioning. Scope changes return for the affected reviews.
Change Management evaluates change governance, not the technical correctness of
each command or mapping. No universal approval turnaround SLA is imposed.

## 6. Cloud and On-premises Privileged Access

### 6.1 Cloud-managed Infrastructure

**Proposed:** Cloud Platform owns technical permission mappings and cloud
implementation within delegation. Assess Azure RBAC and GCP authorization at
the intended scope; avoid interpreting an infrastructure assignment as unlimited
administration, database permission, or production deployment approval.

Review AKS/cloud permissions and Kubernetes RBAC for their separate effective
capabilities. Cloud access does not establish that every Kubernetes action is
authorized; do not default to cluster-wide administration for troubleshooting.
For Key Vault, distinguish managing vault infrastructure from retrieving or
managing its secrets, keys, and certificates. Approve and verify the actual
data-access scope as well as administrative scope without exposing values.

Platform implements cloud networking/VNet/ingress access and automation controls
with Security and IAM governance. An identity or group creation requires §2.2
delegation; a cloud console's creation capability is not that delegation.

### 6.2 Data Center and Database Boundaries

**Proposed:** Data Center teams implement their Windows/Active Directory
infrastructure, Linux privileged access, on-premises server/Jenkins, network
device/routing, and local identity integration controls. Workforce directory
records and group-management governance remain with IAM. Distinguish operating
AD infrastructure from authority to create employees, change corporate groups,
or approve access; record any delegated administration explicitly.

Database teams validate and implement database permissions and administrative
changes under the applicable approvals, regardless of hosting location. Cloud
Platform or Data Center teams supply underlying infrastructure dependencies;
hosting a database does not automatically confer authority over its data or
database roles. Record cross-team handoffs and verified completion for each layer.

The cloud/on-premises distinction governs implementation ownership, not weaker
security controls. Detailed Windows, Linux, network, and database procedures
remain separate technical designs and future runbooks.

## 7. Managed Identities and Workload Federation

### 7.1 Workload Ownership and Trust

**Proposed:** Each service/workload identity has accountable business and
technical owners, an approved purpose, environment, permissions, and lifecycle
record consistent with proposed IT-001 §8. Prefer managed identities or workload
federation where supported and appropriate. Security reviews trust boundaries;
the technical owner validates allowed issuers, audiences, subjects/workloads,
and target permissions in a controlled design, not in public policy examples.

Federation or a managed identity does not approve its permissions automatically.
Platform implements cloud-managed identities and trust integrations under IAM
and Security delegation. Data Center teams implement their workload integrations;
database-specific permissions remain with database owners. Avoid broad trust
matching or permission reuse beyond the approved workload purpose.

### 7.2 Jenkins and CI/CD Identities

**Proposed:** Cloud Platform administers cloud-hosted Jenkins infrastructure and
its technical identity integrations; Data Center teams administer on-premises
Jenkins and server integrations. Application teams retain application-code,
artifact, and application-validation responsibilities. IAM and Security govern
the identities and controls in both locations.

Use attributable workload identities scoped to the required targets and actions.
Do not use employees' personal credentials as standing CI/CD authentication.
Where a credential is necessary, record why and apply §8. A pipeline crossing
cloud/on-premises boundaries needs recorded owners for both the source integration
and target permissions. Protected deployment gates and the separate production
approval process remain applicable; possession of a deployment identity does not
authorize a release.

## 8. Secrets and Credential Management

### 8.1 Storage, Use, and Exposure Prevention

**Proposed:** Security sets credential requirements and reviews the handling
design; the owning technical team implements approved storage, access restrictions,
delivery, and verification. Use an approved secret-management mechanism rather
than source code, scripts containing embedded values, tickets, or general logs.
Restrict who and what can retrieve a credential and separate secret references
from secret values in documentation and pipeline configuration.

Design CI/CD execution and diagnostic output to avoid revealing passwords,
tokens, private keys, or secret values. Log masking alone is not sufficient
evidence of safe handling; review how inputs, artifacts, and outputs are exposed.
Key Vault infrastructure ownership does not grant unrestricted secret retrieval.

### 8.2 Lifecycle and Suspected Exposure

**Proposed:** Record credential owner, purpose, dependencies, issuance/expiry
conditions, and the approved renewal, rotation, and revocation design. Security
and technical owners agree risk-based schedules and triggers; this draft supplies
no arbitrary rotation period or universal token lifetime.

On suspected exposure, engage Security for containment and technical owners for
authorized revocation/replacement and dependency validation. Record unresolved
impact and coordinate with SRE when service reliability is affected. A personnel
change triggers ownership review under proposed IT-001 §8.3, not automatic
shutdown of every shared workload or permission to retain the person's access.

## 9. Temporary and Emergency Access

### 9.1 Planned Temporary Troubleshooting

**Proposed:** Temporary production troubleshooting access needs a named subject,
specific purpose and scope, applicable §5 approvals, MFA controls for human use,
explicit approved start/end times, monitoring, and a revocation owner. Record
how expiry will be enforced and verified across affected systems.

Choose duration from the justified task and assessed risk; no fixed duration is
assumed. Extension requires renewed authorization before relying on it. Access
expiry and the production change window are separate constraints; extending one
does not extend the other. Do not leave a grant active merely because the ticket
or incident remains open.

### 9.2 Emergency Access During Incidents

**Proposed:** Record the incident, urgency, minimum required scope, named user or
workload, business justification, Security decision, technical executor, explicit
end/revocation conditions, monitoring, and subsequent review owner. SRE supplies
incident context and restoration coordination; IAM coordinates identity actions;
the delegated technical team executes authorized grants and removals.

Incident urgency, severity, or Rapid Response participation does not automatically
permit privileged access or MFA bypass. Emergency access needs explicit authority
under a reviewed emergency design; a Security reviewer must not be inferred to
have unrestricted emergency powers from job title alone. Any MFA/control exception
also needs the explicit Security decision and safeguards in §14.

Actual emergency approvers, fallback authentication, permissible abbreviated
business/technical steps, enforcement, and follow-up arrangements remain to be
agreed with IAM, technical owners, SRE, and Change Management. This draft does
not provide an executable break-glass procedure. A production intervention still
needs the applicable separate change authorization under proposed PLAT-001
§§11.1–11.2; retrospective records alone do not authorize an earlier action.
Rapid Response has no newly assigned window-extension authority here.

## 10. Access Monitoring and Revocation

### 10.1 Monitoring and Review

**Proposed:** Security defines detection and review requirements; IAM and technical
teams provide attributable records of privileged sign-ins, MFA/control changes,
permission and group changes, delegated administration, and workload/credential
use where supported. Record monitoring gaps and obtain a reviewed treatment
rather than assume coverage. Route suspicious activity to Security and preserve
sanitized evidence. Exact tooling, alert thresholds, and review schedules require
agreement; COMP-001 proposes evidence governance without adopted review schedules.

### 10.2 Coordinated Removal and Verification

**Proposed:** IAM coordinates lifecycle access removal across cloud, on-premises,
and database layers. Platform removes cloud-managed assignments and delegated
identities/groups; Data Center teams remove their system and integration access;
database teams remove database grants. IAM retains responsibility for enterprise
workforce records and directory/group administration within its governance.

Verify effective removal, including inherited/group access, local accounts,
sessions, and tokens where supported. Central account disablement must not be
assumed to revoke every downstream session. Record incomplete removal and
escalate to the technical owner and Security; uncertainty is not completion.
Technical details and supported revocation coverage must be agreed for each
system. Temporary-access expiry requires verification even if an incident closes.

HR-001 §§4.2–4.4 and 5.1–5.2 remain authoritative for notification and removal
deadlines, including the qualified planned-departure notice and approved
termination effective time. This policy adds no HR deadline, grace period,
business-day convention, or incident exception to termination disablement.
Replace workload ownership or plan retirement under IT-001 §8.3 separately;
service continuity does not justify retaining a departing person's access.

## 11. Security Incident Coordination

**Proposed:** Report suspected credential misuse, unauthorized privilege changes,
MFA manipulation, or certificate/private-key compromise to the designated Security
response function. Security owns security assessment, containment requirements,
and security-sensitive recovery decisions. Technical owners execute authorized
actions on their systems; Security governance does not require Security to perform
every permission change.

For concurrent reliability impact, link records and coordinate with the SRE
incident coordinator under proposed SRE-001 §§5.2 and 11. SRE coordinates
restoration priorities while Security owns security controls. Do not override
containment for service restoration without an authorized decision. Resolve
conflicts through documented governance; maintain separate change/access decisions
and record actions, verification, and residual risks.

The Security responder roster, security incident classification, joint-command
and conflict-escalation process, external communications authority, and detailed
response runbooks remain to be agreed. SRE's reliability severity scale does not
define security severity. This section proposes requirements and ownership,
not a complete security-incident response playbook or legal notification schedule.

## 12. PKI and Certificate Governance

### 12.1 Security Standards and Delegated Operations

**Proposed:** Security owns PKI and certificate governance, including permitted
issuance and renewal controls, trust requirements, private-key protection, risk
assessment, and Venafi-related controls. Record a certificate/service inventory
with accountable service owner, technical administrator, approved names/purpose,
environment, issuer/trust design, expiry information, and renewal/revocation
responsibilities. Do not place private keys in the policy or ordinary records.

Cloud Platform implements cloud certificate integrations; Data Center teams
implement their on-premises server/network integrations; application and database
teams validate use in their services. Designate the actual certificate-service
administrator and any Venafi platform operator explicitly. Security's standards
ownership does not imply that Security operates every integration or that
Platform administers every on-premises certificate.

### 12.2 Issuance, Renewal, and Failures

**Proposed:** Validate the requester's authority, certificate purpose/names,
trust boundary, key protection, and target integration against reviewed Security
requirements before issuance or renewal. Limit administrative/API permissions
and verify installation, trust, and application connectivity without exposing
private keys. Record outcomes, expiry monitoring, ownership handoffs, and failed
renewal escalation. Venafi automation does not substitute for authorization.

Security and technical owners agree issuance, validity, renewal, key-replacement,
and revocation rules based on the approved design; no universal validity or
renewal period is invented. For failures or suspected compromise, engage the
certificate-service administrator, Security, and affected technical owners;
involve SRE for service impact and use the applicable production change process.
Do not disable certificate validation or substitute unreviewed trust to hide a
failure. Actual issuers, algorithms, operating assignments, and procedures remain
implementation decisions, not assertions about a live Venafi deployment.

## 13. Security Evidence and Auditability

**Proposed:** Preserve traceable references to the subject/workload, business
purpose, risk classification, permissions requested and applied, applicable
business/Security/change decisions, delegation, technical executor, timestamps
with time-zone context, MFA/control verification, expiry/removal evidence,
exceptions, incident links, and credential/certificate lifecycle actions.

Use protected evidence references and sanitized summaries; never include secret
values or private keys as proof. Record unsuccessful or partial actions and
monitoring gaps accurately. Security defines security evidence needs, IAM maintains
central identity/delegation records, and technical owners supply execution evidence.
Compliance governs retention, review, and disposal through proposed COMP-001;
this draft establishes no retention duration or compliance certification.

## 14. Exceptions

**Proposed:** A security exception records the affected requirement, justification,
subject/system scope, assessed risk, compensating controls, responsible owner,
explicit expiry, approving authority, decision, monitoring, and expiry/remediation
action. An authorized Security risk decision-maker may approve only within
documented authority, with affected IAM/technical/business owners engaged.
The actual decision-maker and delegation limits require confirmation.

IT, Platform, Data Center, database, SRE, or Change Management approval alone
cannot waive Security requirements. A technical administrator's implementation
rights are not exception authority. Security approval likewise does not replace
business approval, IAM delegation, production change authorization, or another
owner's technical review. Missing control approval blocks reliance on the exception.

Exceptions cannot extend HR-001 employee termination deadlines, create an automatic
MFA bypass, approve a deployment-window extension, or rewrite Compliance governance.
Renewal requires a new recorded decision; expiry is not silently extended by an
open incident. This proposed process is not effective while SEC-001 remains draft.

## 15. Policy Ownership and Review

**Proposed:** The Head of Information Security owns this policy and coordinates
review with IT/IAM, Cloud Platform, Data Center, database/application owners,
SRE, Change Management, and Compliance. Review is annual and triggered by material
security incidents, infrastructure/delegation changes, or companion-policy updates.

An authorized technology governance approver approves publication after required
Security and stakeholder reviews. Confirm that authority and assign an effective
date only after approval; no committee or existing publication approval is assumed.
The proposed owner title and publication process also require review. Publication
does not itself approve individual grants, operational delegations, or unresolved
emergency controls. Version 0.1 remains Draft — Proposed (Synthetic).
