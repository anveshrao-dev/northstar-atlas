## 1. Policy Purpose

SEC-001 proposes a common Security control model for Northstar Technologies'
fictional hybrid infrastructure. It separates standards and risk ownership,
enterprise identity governance, delegated technical implementation, and production
change authorization. This prevents cloud capability or local administrator
access from being mistaken for unrestricted approval power.

The draft adds explicit Azure/GCP and on-premises ownership, human MFA proposals,
workload/credential requirements, temporary and emergency access safeguards,
revocation coordination, and PKI governance. It covers all requested technical
scenarios through ownership and control requirements, without commands, resource
IDs, live configurations, or implementation of a RAG application.

## 3. Ownership Model

| Layer or decision | Owner | Practical boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standards, risk, MFA, credential/PKI requirements, security exceptions | Information Security | Governs controls while technical administration can be delegated |
| Enterprise identities, workforce groups, lifecycle coordination, delegation records | IT Operations / IAM | Corporate identity governance stays central even when systems or directories are operated by another team |
| Cloud implementation | Cloud Platform Engineering | Implements Azure/GCP, RBAC, Kubernetes, Key Vault, cloud networking, cloud identities/federation, and cloud Jenkins within recorded authority |
| On-premises implementation | Data Center Infrastructure teams | Operate Windows/AD infrastructure, Linux, on-premises Jenkins, networks, and integrations without automatically inheriting workforce identity or approval authority |
| Database administration and permissions | Database teams | Coordinate hosting dependencies but retain database-specific technical responsibility |
| Business access need | Manager/receiving manager or workload business owner | Does not substitute for Security approval or technical execution |
| Production change authorization | Change Management | Does not technically approve Terraform plans, RBAC mappings, or each command |
| Reliability incidents | SRE, with responsible technical teams | Coordination does not imply Security authorization or ownership of every fix |

SEC-001 §2.2 requires explicit IAM and Security approval of delegated identity/group
administration. The record identifies permitted actions, scope, people, conditions,
review/expiry, logging, and withdrawal. It does not preapprove each access grant or
allow the administrator to approve their own request. IT-001's standard-access
catalogue remains with IAM; actual profiles and technical maps still require review.

The model is component-specific. Cloud-hosted Jenkins belongs to Platform;
on-premises Jenkins belongs to Data Center teams. A pipeline may cross both and
needs explicit source-integration and target-permission owners. Operating AD
infrastructure differs from governing employee records and workforce groups.
Database authority is not inferred from owning the host.

## 5. Proposed Security Decisions

The entire policy remains proposed. These decisions require review before adoption.

| Decision | Proposed control and policy sections | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D01 — Hybrid governance and delegation | Central Security/IAM governance with explicitly approved, recorded technical delegation (§2) | Allows local expertise without uncontrolled identity or approval authority |
| D02 — Risk and least privilege | Assess effective scope, inheritance, escalation, and data exposure; sensitive/privileged controls can overlap; separate attributed administrative use (§3) | Role names and hosting labels alone do not establish risk |
| D03 — Human MFA | Require MFA for human privileged access; prefer phishing-resistant methods; govern enrollment/recovery and legacy exceptions (§4) | Avoids silent password-only fallback while leaving actual approved methods/designs for review |
| D04 — Approval and provisioning | Keep business approval, Security authorization, technical fulfillment, and applicable production change authorization distinct; prohibit requester self-approval (§5) | Permission capability and urgency are not authority |
| D05 — Layered implementation | Distinguish Azure/GCP, Kubernetes, Key Vault data access, AD infrastructure, Linux, and database responsibilities (§6) | Prevents host ownership from becoming blanket access approval |
| D06 — Workload authentication | Named owners, approved trust/permissions, managed identity/federation preference, and hosting-specific Jenkins integrations (§7) | Avoids personal standing credentials and unbounded federation trust |
| D07 — Secret lifecycle | Approved storage/delivery, restricted retrieval, exposure prevention, and risk-based renewal/revocation design (§8) | Keeps values out of code, logs, and evidence without inventing rotation periods |
| D08 — Temporary/emergency access | Explicit scope, times, approvals, expiry verification, and separate change authorization; no usable break-glass procedure until governance/design gaps close (§9) | Access expiry, incident status, and change windows remain separate |
| D09 — Monitoring and removal | Security requirements with IAM/technical evidence; verify cross-system removal and escalate incomplete revocation without extending HR deadlines (§10) | Central disablement alone may not establish complete removal |
| D10 — Security incidents and PKI | Security owns requirements; specialists execute; explicit certificate inventory, issuance/renewal decisions, delegated integrations, and escalation (§§11–12) | Clarifies governance without claiming a complete response playbook or deployed Venafi service |
| D11 — Evidence, exceptions, and publication | Sanitized attributable records; bounded Security exception authority; owner-led review and authorized governance publication (§§13–15) | Preserves other owners' decisions and pending effectiveness |

No actual role mapping, administrator delegation, MFA product configuration,
credential rotation period, privileged-access duration, certificate validity
period, or universal approval SLA is established by these proposals.

## 6. Alternatives and Tradeoffs

| Choice | Proposed approach | Alternative and tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Technical administration | Recorded delegation to cloud, data center, and database specialists | Central IAM/Security execution of every change simplifies one queue but can obscure specialist ownership and increase handoffs |
| Privileged access | Narrow scope with temporary elevation preferred for troubleshooting | Continuing privileges can support justified duties but require continuing ownership and risk review; no blanket duration is imposed |
| MFA adoption | A reviewed privileged authentication path, phishing-resistant methods preferred, governed legacy exceptions | Silent password-only fallback is not acceptable; replacing or integrating legacy systems offers stronger consistency but requires implementation planning |
| Workload authentication | Managed identities/federation where supported, reviewed credential alternatives otherwise | Stored credentials can support incompatible systems but require additional storage, exposure, renewal, and revocation controls |
| Certificate operations | Security standards with explicit hosting-specific integrators and a named service administrator | Central execution of every certificate action can simplify uniformity but may create a bottleneck; unrestricted team issuance would weaken governance |

These are review tradeoffs, not alternate effective procedures or instructions
to deploy a particular security product.

## 9. RAG Ingestion and Citation Implications

- Preserve document ID, title, version, status, owner, department, dates,
  classification, language, source path, and content hash. Draft date is not
  effectiveness, and deliverable acceptance is not policy publication.
- Attach proposed status to SEC/IT/PLAT/SRE chunks. Approved-rule retrieval should
  exclude drafts by default; draft-review answers must disclose status. Keep
  this review report outside the operational policy corpus.
- Retrieve ownership with environment, component, identity type, and action.
  “Jenkins” alone is insufficient to choose Platform versus Data Center ownership;
  “Active Directory” does not erase the IAM/infrastructure distinction.
- Keep delegation conditions with technical powers in §2.2 and all four decisions
  together in §5.2. Do not turn implementation authority into approval authority.
- Preserve human MFA versus workload authentication, sensitive versus privileged,
  Key Vault management versus data access, and database roles versus host access.
  These distinctions prevent plausible but overbroad answers.
- Keep emergency/exception conditions and unresolved authority with §§9 and 14.
  No precise MFA fallback, duration, rotation schedule, or certificate operator
  may be fabricated to complete an answer.
- Resolve document presence separately from approval and rule completeness.
  SEC-001 now exists as a draft; COMP-001 remains absent. Older “not yet created”
  wording must not outweigh the actual file manifest, nor imply the new rules
  are effective. Flag broader hybrid-scope alignment as a review dependency.
- Use cross-document citations for cross-team decisions, such as SEC-001 v0.1
  §5.2 with PLAT-001 v0.1 §4.2. Cite HR-001 for deadlines and state what the
  corpus cannot support. Check evidence and qualifiers, not just keyword overlap.
