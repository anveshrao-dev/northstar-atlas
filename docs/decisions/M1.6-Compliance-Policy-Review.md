## 1. Policy Purpose

COMP-001 proposes internal evidence governance for the fictional Northstar
Technologies. Its purpose is to connect requests, authorized decisions, actual
execution, and verified results across access, lifecycle, deployment, and incident
processes. Compliance governs requirements and oversight while source owners
remain accountable for the evidence they produce.

The proposed lifecycle is identify/classify records, collect linked evidence,
review completeness and access, protect provenance and corrections, provide
authorized scoped access, and retain/dispose under an approved schedule and hold
status. Evidence ownership does not authorize the underlying action. No regulatory
retention period, audit certification, or legal requirement is fabricated.

## 3. Ownership Boundaries

| Owner | Accountable evidence | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compliance | Catalogue, review requirements, retention standards, completeness oversight, audit coordination | Does not approve production actions or waive other owners' controls |
| IT/IAM | Workforce access/lifecycle coordination, inventories, central delegation and reconciled removal evidence | Specialist system logs remain with technical owners |
| HR | Employee event, approval, notification, and lifecycle records | Does not own or execute every access removal |
| Platform | Cloud implementation, Terraform technical-review/plan, and deployment execution evidence | Does not replace final Change Management authorization |
| Data Center and database teams | Their system-specific access, actions, and verification records | Host and database responsibilities remain separate |
| Security | Security decisions, privileged-access controls, exceptions, and expiry review evidence | Technical teams perform and document system actions |
| Change Management | Production authorization, scope/window/conditions, and amendments | Does not technically approve Terraform plans |
| SRE | Coordinated incident timeline, communications, restoration/closure, and learning records | Technical and Security owners retain their own action/decision evidence |
| Managers/application/service owners | Continuing business-need attestations and application approvals/validation | Review attestation does not create a new grant |

The model uses accountable source owners with a controlled catalogue/index rather
than requiring unrestricted copies in a central repository. Temporary privileged
expiry has explicit handoffs: technical owners verify removal, IAM reconciles it,
Security reviews privileged conditions/exceptions, and Compliance checks evidence
completeness and unresolved findings (COMP-001 §6.2).

## 5. Proposed Decisions Requiring Approval

All COMP-specific requirements remain subject to review. This register is not
an approval record.

| Decision | Proposal and sections | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D01 — Catalogue and ownership | Accountable owners, protected sources, sensitivity and schedule references (§§2–3) | Avoids ownerless evidence and unnecessary duplication |
| D02 — Access reviews | Risk-based planned reviews plus event-driven actions; business/Security/technical responsibilities; independent review of own privileged access (§4) | Separates review oversight from granting or removing access |
| D03 — Lifecycle and expiry | Linked HR/IAM/technical evidence; explicit temporary privileged expiry verification and Security review (§§5–7) | A planned expiry, central disablement, or closed ticket is not proof of complete removal |
| D04 — Deployment evidence | Link CR/cTASK readiness and final authorization to Platform plan review, applied artifact, execution, and outcome (§8) | Proves both authorized intent and actual execution without conflating reviewers |
| D05 — Incident evidence | Retain timeline, decisions, verification, residual impact, closure and follow-up records under schedule/hold governance (§9) | Closure does not erase evidence or prove all work finished |
| D06 — Integrity | Traceable sources/versions, controlled corrections, honest gaps and reconstructed-record labels (§10) | Prevents backdating, fabricated approval, and silent history changes |
| D07 — Retention/disposal | Versioned category schedules with documented purpose, start event, period/rationale, approval, holds, and verified disposal (§11) | Establishes governance without inventing legal or arbitrary numeric periods |
| D08 — Audit access | Scoped requests, owner/Security assessment, authorized release, sanitized packages, and controlled inspection when justified (§12) | Auditor status does not grant raw-secret or production access |
| D09 — Exceptions/holds | Bounded Compliance exceptions, recorded preservation assessment, authorized hold scope/release, custodian acknowledgment (§13) | Separates exceptions, preservation, and lawful-authority questions without asserting a legal obligation |
| D10 — Publication | Proposed owner, annual/change-triggered review, required stakeholders, authorized governance approver and later effective date (§14) | Separates policy publication from operational decisions |

No numeric retention period is needed to define this governance draft. Schedules
must supply justified values and review plans must supply agreed cadences before
operational adoption. Leaving these explicit is preferable to making unsupported
values look authoritative; it is not a permission for uncontrolled deletion or
indefinite retention.

## 6. Alternatives and Tradeoffs

| Choice | Proposed approach | Alternative and tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evidence storage | Protected owner-held sources with a controlled catalogue/index | Central copies simplify collection but duplicate sensitive data and can become stale; either design still needs integrity and access controls |
| Review planning | Risk-based periodic plans with event-driven action | A universal interval is simpler but ignores differing risks and could be mistaken for an existing obligation; none is selected here |
| Retention | Approved category schedules with purpose, trigger, and rationale | One duration for all records is easy to administer but may over-retain some records and under-retain others; no legal period is inferred |
| Audit delivery | Sanitized evidence first, justified controlled inspection where needed | Bulk raw exports simplify transfer but expose secrets and unnecessary data; an auditor's request does not approve them |
| Corrections | Attributable changes with preserved history | Silent overwrites are simpler but remove the ability to reconstruct what was known and approved at the time |

These are design options and tradeoffs, not deployed systems or approved alternate
procedures. Retention and hold implementation still requires the decisions below.

## 9. RAG Ingestion and Citation Implications

- Preserve document/version/section IDs, owner, department, classification,
  status, dates, source path, and content hash. Pending approval is not an
  effective date; project acceptance is not policy publication.
- Keep COMP/IT/PLAT/SRE/SEC draft labels on chunks. Approved-policy retrieval
  should exclude drafts by default; review-mode answers must disclose status.
  Keep this report outside the operational policy corpus.
- Preserve actor, authority, action, and evidence type together. Evidence owner,
  custodian, reviewer, approver, and executor are distinct roles even when one
  person has documented authority in more than one role.
- Keep Terraform evidence chains and regeneration conditions together; retrieve
  Platform technical approval alongside separate Change Management authorization.
  Preserve the difference between recorded intent and verified outcomes.
- Keep sensitive-audit limits with request procedures. A reference to protected
  evidence is not permission to ingest its secret-bearing contents into a future
  vector store. Sensitive operational evidence needs its own access design.
- Retention answers must distinguish schedule fields from approved values,
  internal standards from separately verified obligations, and ordinary expiry
  from holds. Do not invent a duration, legal trigger, or legal authority to fill
  an unresolved field. Annual policy review is not record retention.
- Treat missing, reconstructed, or contradictory evidence as answer limitations;
  a hash establishes neither business authorization nor truth by itself.
- Reconcile actual file presence with current metadata independently of stale
  dependency text. All six files exist, but five remain drafts and operational
  schedules/delegations are incomplete. Existence is not approval or readiness.
- Use cross-document citations, such as COMP-001 v0.1 §8.2 with PLAT-001 v0.1
  §6.1. Cite HR-001 for HR deadlines; evaluation should test unsupported-answer
  handling as well as relevant retrieval.
