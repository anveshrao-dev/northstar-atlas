## 1. Policy Purpose

PLAT-001 proposes production change governance for Northstar Technologies,
a fictional enterprise SaaS company. It connects business intent, security
assessment, access capability, production authorization, technical execution,
monitoring, recovery, and evidence without collapsing them into one approval.

The draft covers Terraform changes to Azure, AKS configuration, Helm releases,
production configuration, protected CI/CD environments, Kubernetes permissions,
workload identities, and Azure Key Vault integration. These are governance
requirements and illustrative contexts, not executable procedures or real
infrastructure definitions. All newly introduced rules remain proposed.

This revision applies the requested Platform-led deployment operating model.
The previous draft assigned production approval to an engineering manager or
generic change authority, did not define a CR/cTASK preparation sequence, and
could be read as requiring renewed change approval whenever a Terraform plan
was regenerated. It also left application deployment ownership too implicit.
Those gaps obscured the distinction between technical implementation decisions
and coordinated production authorization.

The revised model makes Platform the primary infrastructure technical owner,
retains developers' application responsibilities, and assigns final CR approval
to Change Management after the required preparation and applicable approvals.
It does not turn project review or this revision into policy publication.

## 4. Ownership and Approval Boundaries

| Decision or activity | Proposed owner | What it does not authorize |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business purpose and application release approval | Requester supplies purpose; application manager records release and validation approvals within their remit | Final CR approval, privileged access, or a Security waiver |
| Application code, artifacts, and application validation | Developers and application/service owner | Ownership of all platform infrastructure or automatic production authorization |
| Infrastructure design, Terraform technical approval, and implementation method | Platform Engineering / Cloud / DevOps; authorized Platform reviewer approves the production plan | Final CR approval or a Security waiver; Change Management does not select Terraform versus manual work |
| Security conditions and assessment | Information Security | Automatic deployment execution |
| Identity administration and access coordination | IT Operations, with Platform-owned technical permission implementation | A production change merely because access was provisioned |
| Final production CR approval | Authorized Change Management approver after required implementation plans, prerequisite completion, nonproduction evidence, and applicable approvals | Terraform technical approval, new credentials, or execution outside approved scope/risk/window |
| Infrastructure execution and technical rollback | Platform Engineering and the named authorized executor | Ownership of every application deployment step, self-authorization, or incident command |
| Application, database, and network execution/recovery | Responsible teams according to recorded cTASK sequence and handoffs | Unreviewed scope or completion merely because a task was assigned |
| Operational readiness and incident coordination | SRE, with service-owner input | Blanket authority to bypass deployment or security controls |
| Urgent assistance and window-extension coordination | Proposed Rapid Response function | Automatic extension approval, a Security exception, or replacement of SRE incident command |
| Evidence governance | Compliance | An invented retention period or claim of completed audit compliance |

Application-manager approval and Platform technical approval do not substitute
for Change Management's final CR decision. The requester cannot be the sole
change approver; normal production Terraform changes also separate plan author
from technical reviewer. Actual role assignments and delegations remain subject
to review; no named governance committee or new department policy is invented.

### 4.1 Revised End-to-End Deployment Lifecycle

1. Business requests a release or engineering identifies a necessary change.
2. Developers prepare code, artifacts, and application validation; Platform
   prepares the deployment infrastructure for Dev, QA, UAT, and Production.
3. Developers and Platform coordinate nonproduction deployments. Application,
   Platform, database, and network owners resolve their issues and supply
   validation evidence. Record application-manager approvals and applicable
   Security and SRE readiness reviews.
4. Build the CR while preparation proceeds. Link Platform and application cTASKs,
   applicable database/network tasks, implementation plans and sequence, contacts,
   dependencies/risks, verification and recovery steps, and proposed window.
5. Complete required prerequisite tasks and record applicable approvals, including
   Platform approval of the production Terraform plan. Change Management then
   reviews readiness, coordination, risk, documentation, and window and records
   final CR approval. Production execution tasks are planned here, not falsely
   recorded as already complete. Standard-template use still requires the
   CR-specific readiness and final approval in PLAT-001 §4.4.
6. Recheck go/no-go conditions. Platform executes infrastructure work; developers
   execute or coordinate application release work; database and network teams
   perform their tasks in the agreed sequence and authorized window.
7. Verify both infrastructure and application health, record failures/deviations,
   and use authorized recovery when needed. Rapid Response coordinates urgent
   assistance or extension requests; it cannot grant authority by coordination.
   Close only with verified outcomes and recorded evidence.

This separates early CR preparation, completion of prerequisites, final approval,
and actual production execution. Infrastructure-only changes explain inapplicable
application steps and retain relevant validation and readiness requirements.
An emergency route cannot skip these controls without the separately agreed
authority and abbreviated process still pending SRE-001 and SEC-001.

### 4.2 Terraform Plan and Drift Decisions

Platform prepares a production-targeted plan, reviews creates/updates/replacements/
deletions and dependencies, records approval by an authorized Platform reviewer,
and applies that reviewed plan to its bound target through an authorized process.
Normal production changes preserve author/reviewer separation. State protection,
artifact integrity, Security decisions, and recovery feasibility still apply.

Drift or plan regeneration triggers Platform investigation, corrections, renewed
technical review/approval, and a recorded comparison against the approved CR.
If the resulting scope, assessed risk, window, and other conditions remain within
authorization, no separate Change Management approval is required solely for
regeneration. The regenerated plan still needs its own technical approval.
Material scope/risk expansion or a window overrun stops affected execution until
the appropriate amendment or escalation yields an authorized decision. Neither
drift nor an escalation request is automatic permission to proceed.

## 7. Alternatives and Tradeoffs

| Choice | Proposed approach | Alternative and tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repeatable change authorization | Reuse reviewed template evidence with CR-specific prerequisite and final approval records | Re-review every technical detail for each release: simpler eligibility logic but more review work; template reuse requires maintained boundaries and cannot waive readiness |
| Approval organization | Platform technical approval followed by Change Management's final readiness/governance approval | A generic manager approving both technical and production decisions obscures specialist ownership and cross-team readiness; the revised model makes each decision explicit without inventing a committee |
| Execution route | Protected pipelines with attributable identities | Manual execution can support unusual recovery needs but needs an explicitly authorized, evidenced alternative; it is not a fallback for a failed gate |
| Terraform regeneration | Renew Platform technical approval; retain the existing CR decision when scope, risk, window, and other conditions still hold | Resubmit every regenerated plan for Change Management approval: adds review without distinguishing technical changes from governance changes; silent reuse of an old technical approval is also unacceptable |
| Rollout strategy | Stage according to impact and service criteria | Rolling, canary, or blue/green approaches have different capacity, state, and routing costs; no single strategy is mandated for every workload |
| Recovery strategy | Choose reviewed rollback or roll-forward based on feasibility | Automatic rollback is useful for bounded reversible releases; data migrations or destructive infrastructure changes may instead need tested restoration or forward repair |

These alternatives are design options, not additional approved procedures.
Provider-specific implementations remain future work.
