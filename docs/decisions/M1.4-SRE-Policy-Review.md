## 1. Policy Purpose

SRE-001 proposes a reliability incident lifecycle for the fictional Northstar
Technologies. Its core distinction is between coordinating restoration and owning
each technical intervention. SRE assesses impact and coordinates teams; Platform
owns infrastructure work, applications own code and application validation, and
database/network teams retain their specialisms. Security and Change Management
retain their separate control and authorization responsibilities.

The lifecycle is report and triage, declaration and severity assessment, named
coordination and technical engagement, authorized mitigation, verified restoration,
recorded closure, and proportionate review with tracked corrective actions.
Communication and evidence continue throughout. Detection does not require a
known root cause, and incident closure need not imply completed causal analysis.

The document supplies governance and handoffs for all eight requested incident
contexts, not diagnostic commands, live infrastructure, or a working RAG system.
Every new SRE rule remains proposed.

## 3. Incident Ownership Model

The proposed SRE coordinator owns the shared incident view, prioritization,
engagement, communication, and closure record. Technical leads own the actual
investigation, proposed intervention, execution within authorization, and
component verification. An acknowledged handoff preserves responsibility across
staff changes; merely sending a ticket does not transfer ownership.

| Decision/activity | Owner | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reliability declaration, severity, and coordination | Responding SRE representative and named SRE coordinator, with service-owner input | No automatic production change or access approval |
| Platform infrastructure and Terraform technical controls | Platform Engineering / Cloud / DevOps | Does not own every application fix or replace final CR approval |
| Application code, releases, and application health | Application teams and service owner | Pipeline success alone cannot establish service health |
| Database and networking tasks | Database and DNS/Network teams, with explicit Platform shared-component handoffs | No ownership inferred solely from a symptom or ticket assignment |
| Security-incident controls and privileged access | Information Security, with IT/technical execution under access governance | SRE cannot waive containment or access controls to restore service |
| Production change authorization | Change Management under the revised PLAT-001 proposal | Does not technically approve Terraform plans |
| Urgent assistance and extension-request coordination | Rapid Response under documented authority | No automatic window extension, incident command, or Security exception |
| Evidence governance | Compliance | No invented retention or review schedule |

For an overlapping security/reliability event, link the records and coordinate
decisions while leaving Security in charge of security controls. The proposed
default is that SRE retains reliability coordination during Rapid Response
engagement unless a documented handoff occurs. Formal delegations and the full
joint-command interface remain to be reviewed.

## 5. Proposed Decisions Requiring Approval

All SRE-specific requirements require review; this register groups the material
choices and is not an approval record.

| Decision | Proposal and policy sections | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D01 — Intake and declaration | Report credible production impact without waiting for root cause; responding SRE assesses the report, declares applicable incidents, and records a named coordinator; escalate unavailable ownership (§3) | Reduces delay while avoiding automatic production authorization |
| D02 — Severity | Use Critical, Major, and Limited qualitative categories; record uncertainty and reassess with evidence (§4) | Avoids unsupported universal SLAs and classifying solely by technology |
| D03 — Coordination and handoffs | One named SRE coordinator, distributed technical leads, acknowledged handoffs, and linked Security response (§§2, 5) | Preserves accountability without moving all technical work to SRE |
| D04 — Technical engagement | Use component ownership and scenario handoffs, distinguish hypotheses from facts, and coordinate conflicting work (§6) | Routes failures to the correct teams without embedding runbooks |
| D05 — Escalation and Rapid Response | Escalate qualitative blockers/impact; Rapid Response coordinates help and extension requests; actual approval powers remain unresolved (§7) | Assistance must not become assumed change/window authority |
| D06 — Communication | Assign an owner and impact-based update checkpoints; constrain external publication pending a designated authority (§8) | Provides useful updates without invented intervals, ETAs, or external authority |
| D07 — Restoration and closure | Responsible teams verify service-specific criteria; SRE records closure with residual-risk ownership and review planning; root-cause work can continue (§9) | Distinguishes a successful command, restored service, and completed causal analysis |
| D08 — Change and access | Preserve revised PLAT technical/CR decisions and IT/Security access decisions; emergency details remain dependent (§10) | Prevents incident urgency from creating a bypass |
| D09 — Evidence and learning | Attributable timeline, sanitized evidence, proportionate review for every declared incident, owner/checkpoint/completion evidence for actions (§§11–12) | Makes response and improvement traceable without inventing retention periods |

service criteria, and agreed controls. No numerical acknowledgment, response,

## 6. Alternatives and Tradeoffs

| Design choice | Proposed approach | Alternative and tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Incident ownership | SRE coordinates, specialist teams execute | Making SRE execute every fix simplifies the apparent contact point but obscures technical ownership and creates a response bottleneck |
| Severity | Qualitative impact categories with evidence and reassessment | Numeric availability or response targets can improve measurement once service commitments exist; inventing universal values now would imply unsupported obligations |
| Coordination staffing | One coordinator with named technical leads; communication may share a role for limited incidents | Separate coordinator, communications lead, and recorder for every incident improves role separation but adds staffing overhead; actual staffing should match impact |
| Review scope | A proportionate recorded review for every declared incident | Reviewing only major incidents reduces work but may miss recurring limited failures; this proposal does not require a full workshop for every case |
| Recovery | Choose authorized rollback, forward recovery, or other mitigation based on evidence and feasibility | Default rollback can help reversible releases but may fail for migrations or destructive infrastructure changes; retain PLAT-001's limitations |

These are review alternatives, not effective procedures. Exact implementation,
tools, rosters, technical runbooks, and numerical service targets are future work.

Answers relying on SRE-001, IT-001, or PLAT-001 must disclose draft/proposed
status. Cite HR-001 directly for authoritative lifecycle rules. SEC-001 and
COMP-001 supply no substantive retrievable evidence yet.

| Question | Expected answer and evidence | Unsupported inference to reject |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A Jenkins release fails and AKS pods crash under memory pressure. Does SRE own every fix, and is this automatically Critical? | Proposed SRE-001 §§3.2, 4.1–4.2, 5.1, and 6.1 assign SRE coordination, Platform pipeline/cluster work, and application code/resource investigation; severity follows impact, containment, and workaround evidence. PLAT-001 §9.3 supports the handoffs | The tool name proves severity, SRE must execute all fixes, or this policy promises a numerical response SLA |
| Users lose sessions through NGINX, DNS/load-balancer connectivity fails, and database migrations are suspected. Who is engaged and what can be reported as fact? | Proposed SRE-001 §§6.1–6.2 and 8.1 engage Platform, application, DNS/Network, and database owners according to affected components; SRE coordinates dependencies and updates. Distinguish hypotheses, observed impact, and verified findings | A single symptom proves root cause, the policy supplies troubleshooting commands, or an unacknowledged ticket transfers responsibility |
| Key Vault retrieval or Venafi renewal failures affect production. May SRE grant emergency permissions or bypass Security to restore service? | Proposed SRE-001 §§5.2, 6.1, and 10.2 involve Platform, application/certificate owners, IT, and Security; incident coordination is not access authorization. Proposed IT-001 §§5.2 and 10.2 preserve separate decisions; certificate ownership and detailed Security controls remain unresolved | An incident grants privileged access, the certificate-service owner is already named, or an MFA method/access duration can be inferred from absent SEC-001 |
| A failed deployment needs rollback or forward recovery near the end of its window. Can Rapid Response approve extra time, and does a regenerated Terraform plan always need new Change Management approval? | Proposed SRE-001 §§7.2, 9.1, and 10.1 preserve authorized recovery bounds and route extension requests to an explicitly authorized decision-maker whose identity is unresolved. PLAT-001 §§6.1, 10.1–10.2, and 11.3 preserve renewed Platform technical review for regeneration, no separate CR approval solely for an in-scope result, and amendment/escalation for material expansion or window overrun | Rapid Response automatically approves extra time, recovery has unlimited scope, regeneration needs no technical approval, or every regenerated plan needs a new CR decision |
| The pipeline is green but some user functions still fail. Can the incident be closed, and must the root cause be known first? | Proposed SRE-001 §9.2 requires component/application verification and restoration against agreed criteria, records partial impact, and requires residual-risk ownership and review planning. §§11–12 require evidence and tracked learning; root-cause work may continue after verified restoration and documented closure | Green tooling alone proves restoration, partial restoration equals full resolution, a fixed review deadline/retention period is known, or closure automatically revokes temporary access |

These are review examples, not executed RAG tests or a new evaluation dataset.
Future regression cases should contrast an extension request with an approved
extension, in-scope plan regeneration with material expansion, and administrative
incident closure with verified access expiry. Include an employee-offboarding
case to ensure incident participation never extends HR-001 §5.2's deadline.

## 9. RAG Ingestion and Citation Implications

- Preserve document ID, title, version, owner, department, status, classification,
  language, dates, source path, and content hash. Pending approval is not an
  effective date; project deliverable acceptance is not policy publication.
- Label every SRE/IT/PLAT chunk as proposed. Approved-policy retrieval should
  exclude drafts by default; a review mode can retrieve them with status made
  explicit. Keep this review report outside the operational policy corpus.
- Use heading-aware chunks with stable identifiers and parent context. Keep
  severity criteria with reassessment conditions; a technology example is not
  a severity rule and a category supplies no numerical SLA.
- Preserve actor, action, authority, conditions, and evidence together. “SRE
  coordinates recovery” must not become “SRE approves and executes every fix.”
  Join the scenario owner with the applicable access/change boundaries.
- Keep §7.2's request routing and unresolved extension authority together. Keep
  §10.1's in-scope Terraform rule with renewed technical review and the material
  change exception. Avoid isolated permissive sentences without their limits.
- Distinguish mitigation, restored service, closure, root-cause review, and
  corrective-action completion. Preserve unknown facts and unresolved ownership;
  they are answer boundaries, not blanks to fill with plausible details.
- Resolve references using actual file presence and metadata, separately from
  stale dependency descriptions. SRE-001 now exists as a draft, while SEC-001
  and COMP-001 remain absent. Neither presence nor a reference implies approval.
- Cite actual supporting sections across documents, such as SRE-001 v0.1 §7.2
  with PLAT-001 v0.1 §11.3 for extension requests. Use HR-001 for HR deadlines;
  do not invent authority, SLAs, commands, or controls from absent policies.

No ingestion, embedding, retrieval, application code, or cloud integration was
