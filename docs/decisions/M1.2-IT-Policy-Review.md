## 1. Policy Purpose and Section Design

IT-001 proposes how Northstar Technologies connects a justified business need
to controlled identity creation, entitlement changes, technical execution, and
verifiable evidence. It supports employee identities and service/workload
identities while retaining the six-department ownership boundaries.

The entire IT-specific design remains proposed. Existing HR rules are labeled
separately in the policy so a reader can distinguish inherited authority from
new decisions.

| IT-001 section | Why it exists |
| --- | --- |
| §1 Purpose and Draft Authority | Prevents proposed wording and absent references from being mistaken for approved rules |
| §2 Scope | Defines employee and nonhuman coverage without inventing contractor processes or regulatory compliance |
| §3 Responsibilities and Approval Boundaries | Separates HR, managers, IT, Platform, SRE, Security, and Compliance responsibilities |
| §4 Identity Lifecycle Management | Proposes accountable identity records, onboarding verification, and entitlement lifecycle tracking |
| §5 Access Requests and Standard Provisioning | Distinguishes business approval, Security approval, technical validation, and execution evidence |
| §6 Role-Change Access Modifications | Preserves HR transfer rules while proposing IT coordination of specialist access changes |
| §7 Access Revocation and Offboarding | Preserves HR disablement times and proposes verification across downstream systems |
| §8 Service and Workload Identities | Separates automation identity ownership and credentials from an employee's personal account |
| §9 Cloud and Platform Access Handoffs | Defines IT/Platform execution boundaries and the limited role of SRE operational input |
| §10 Privileged and Emergency Access Referrals | Routes high-risk requests to the proper owners without inventing missing security or incident procedures |
| §11 Access Logging and Audit Evidence | Makes access decisions and execution traceable while leaving retention governance to COMP-001 |
| §12 Exceptions | Proposes a bounded IT exception process without assigning HR exception authority |
| §13 Policy Ownership and Review | Identifies a proposed owner and makes approval and effectiveness explicit |
| §14 Related Policies | Records which documents exist and which dependencies remain planned |

## 3. Responsibilities and Ownership

Business approval answers **why the access is needed**. Security approval
answers **whether the risk and required controls are acceptable**. Technical
provisioning answers **which approved permissions were actually implemented**.
An approval record is not proof of execution, and technical capability is not
approval authority.

The proposed operating model keeps IT as the coordinating owner for identity
administration and standard access. Platform Engineering owns cloud and
deployment-platform permission mappings and platform-owned implementation.
SRE provides context for reliability and incident duties without becoming a
blanket approver for every production request. Security owns sensitive and
privileged access assessment; Compliance owns audit evidence governance.

The catalogue clarification in IT-001 §5.3 assigns central catalogue maintenance
to IT Operations, platform-managed permission mappings to Platform Engineering,
and classification/control review to Information Security. Required Security
and technical-owner reviews must be recorded before a standard profile can be
treated as approved. These responsibilities remain part of the proposed draft.

Managers approve business needs. For a service identity, the accountable
business owner fills that role, while the technical owner maintains its
implementation. HR remains the source of employee lifecycle records and
notifications; it gains no deployment or privileged-access approval authority.

The policy proposes no self-approval and distinguishes the decisions in the
request record. It does not claim a fully specified segregation-of-duties
model or require a different person for every technical step.

## 7. Potential Contradictions and Open Questions

No conflicting HR notification or revocation timeline was identified.
The following boundaries need review before adoption:

- **Previous-role exceptions:** HR-001 anticipates an IT/security exception
  process. IT-001 §12 supplies a proposal, not an effective authorization to
  extend access. The clarified authority is limited to IT-specific exceptions;
  IT approval alone cannot waive Security requirements or extend employee
  termination deadlines. Confirm adoption and safeguards without silently
  weakening HR-001 §4.3.
- **Central versus specialist execution:** HR-001 says IT implements access
  changes; this draft proposes IT coordination with Platform execution for
  platform-owned permissions. Confirm that responsibility split before
  approval; HR-001 was not reinterpreted as transferring ownership to Platform.
- **Production access versus production changes:** Production AKS assessment
  does not approve a deployment, automatically classify every production role
  as administrative, or grant SRE blanket approval authority.
- **Business-day and time-zone ambiguity:** The M1.1 question remains open.
  Timestamp context is proposed for IT evidence; it does not choose HR's
  business calendar or reinterpret HR's effective times.
- **Self-approval and delegation:** Decide who supplies an independent business
  approval when the usual manager or workload business owner is the requester.
  The draft prohibits self-approval but does not invent a delegation hierarchy.
- **Standard access catalogue:** No real catalogue or approved Azure role map
  is supplied. The draft now assigns maintenance, mapping, and security-review
  responsibilities. The actual profiles, classifications, maintenance workflow,
  and recorded reviews remain to be established before the proposed route is used.
- **Service continuity and ownership gaps:** §8.3 avoids automatic service
  shutdown on owner departure and requires an explicit decision for unresolved
  ownership. Define escalation ownership and expectations without delaying
  employee revocation; no numeric response target is proposed here.
- **Future security and evidence rules:** SEC-001, SRE-001, and COMP-001 must
  resolve emergency controls, credential lifetimes, review schedules, and
  retention. The draft cannot answer those details today.
- **Publication:** Confirm the proposed owner/reviewer/approver process and
  identify the authorized technology governance approver and their authority.
  The accepted policy decisions and release version remain to be agreed; an
  effective date is assigned only after publication approval. No named
  governance committee was invented, and no approval or publication occurred.

## 9. Implications for RAG Ingestion and Citations

- Preserve document ID, version, section ID, title, department, owner,
  classification, status, language, and dates. Preserve the distinction between
  draft date and effective date; do not convert pending effectiveness into a
  fabricated date.
- Attach draft status to every IT-001 chunk. Default authoritative-policy
  retrieval should exclude unapproved drafts; an explicit draft-review mode
  can retrieve them with a proposed-status label.
- Distinguish inherited HR rules from IT proposals. If an IT chunk summarizes
  an HR rule, retain its source reference and cite HR-001 for the governing
  deadline; the draft's summary is not an independent approval.
- Keep conditions with their rules: planned versus urgent, “where practicable,”
  employee versus workload, development versus production, and standard versus
  privileged. A chunk containing only a deadline is insufficient.
- Preserve the three decisions in §5.2 together or retain their parent context
  so a business approval is not mistaken for security approval or fulfillment.
- Record cross-document dependencies and their status. The four planned
  policies provide no retrievable evidence for specific controls or schedules.
  Answers requiring them should state what is unsupported.
- Cite document/version/section combinations, such as **IT-001 v0.1 §8.2
  (proposed)**. Preserve source path and content hash for reproducible ingestion.
- Keep this report outside the policy corpus: alternatives, questions, and
  decision-register entries are not approved operational instructions.
