## 9. RAG Ingestion Implications

- Separate **presence**, **approval**, and **completeness**. All six sources exist;
  only HR-001 is marked Approved (Synthetic); five drafts and operational gaps
  remain. Approved-rule answers should cite HR directly for lifecycle obligations.
  A draft-review mode may use the other policies with explicit proposed labels.
- Use the manifest's `policies` array as an explicit source list, rather than
  recursively ingesting historical reviews. `filename` is relative to the repository
  root, declared by `filename_base`; `content_hash` is lowercase SHA-256 of raw
  bytes, declared by `hash_algorithm`. `schema_version` describes the manifest
  format, not policy approval. The manifest is an inventory, not an ingestion tool.
- Preserve document ID, version, source hash, section path, and status on future
  chunks. These edits retain v0.1, so version alone cannot distinguish old and new
  wording. A future index must replace or invalidate superseded content by hash;
  a hash proves a byte match, not truth, authorship, or approval.
- The requested manifest fields are a minimum inventory. Owner, organization,
  review cycle, and section identifiers remain available in the source and should
  be retained by future ingestion. Null effectiveness must not become a draft date.
- Keep actor, environment, action, authority, conditions, and exceptions together.
  Jenkins hosting context determines the technical owner; job title alone does
  not grant access. Keep Terraform's renewed technical approval and CR conditions
  together so a sentence about no extra CR decision cannot become no review.
- Preserve qualifiers such as planned versus urgent, “where practicable,” current
  versus previous access, human versus workload, requested versus approved, and
  executed versus verified. Use multi-document citations when a decision spans
  lifecycle, access, deployment, incident, and evidence responsibilities.
- Unresolved authorities, schedules, procedures, and contractor scope are answer
  limits. A useful answer identifies supported principles and the missing decision
  instead of inventing an approver, timer, legal period, or runbook.
- Classification and evidence references do not authorize ingestion of sensitive
  operational records. Protected Terraform state, secrets, and underlying personal
  records are not part of this six-document synthetic policy manifest.

No parser, embeddings, vector database, retrieval logic, or application behavior
was implemented. These implications guide later engineering; they are not claims
that RAG evaluation has already passed.
